# People Inside the Atmosphere

Use this reference whenever people appear. Preserve the source facts, then allocate painterly description by apparent size. A figure belongs to the same light and air as the landscape; it must not become a separately protected digital subject.

## Preserve the Factual Skeleton

- Lock person count, placement, scale, body direction, pose, overlap, and complete visible extent.
- Preserve identity-critical silhouette, hairstyle, clothing-color families, and meaningful props such as hats, umbrellas, bicycles, chairs, bags, or railings.
- Preserve faces when they are large enough to carry identity. Do not invent facial features for figures that are too small to show them.
- Keep scene-level randomness at zero: do not add people, swap sides, alter gestures, change clothing, or repair anatomy by changing the pose.

## Spend Detail by Distance

### Distant or tiny figures

- Use roughly two to five connected marks: a vertical or leaning body mass, one head or hat note, and an optional limb, prop, or reflected accent.
- Let value contrast, gesture, spacing, and color identify the figure; omit eyes, fingers, seams, and facial anatomy.
- Group crowds into irregular dark-light rhythms while keeping the source count and major spacing readable. Do not resolve every person equally.

### Midground figures

- Build the body from connected warm and cool planes rather than outlined limbs.
- Preserve the pose through weight, lean, shoulder-hip direction, and one or two clothing or prop anchors.
- Keep only the information the scene needs: one overall hair mass, one or two body or garment planes, and meaningful anchors such as a bag, phone, umbrella, hat, or bicycle. Omit strand-by-strand hair, polished arm shading, fingers, seams, printed dots, and other photographic micro-description unless identity depends on them.
- Treat clothing mass before tailoring. Preserve garment silhouette, length, color family, hem direction, and movement, then build it from roughly two to four connected light-and-shadow planes with only one or two broad fold gestures. Omit repeated pleats, ruffles, lace, scallops, seams, and sharp dark grooves unless they are identity-critical.
- Allow foliage, water light, ground color, or air to cross selected edges. Keep one decisive edge or dark core where the pose needs an anchor.

### Foreground and identity-critical figures

- Preserve likeness, expression, proportions, hands, legs, shoes, and complete framing.
- Model face and skin with a few connected warm-cool planes; reserve small accents for essential eyes, brows, mouth, or hair breaks.
- Use broader directional strokes in clothing. Let folds follow the pose without tracing every wrinkle or using smooth digital gradients.

## Integrate Figures With the Scene

- Carry ambient sky, water, foliage, and ground color into clothing, skin, hair, and selected contour breaks at altered value.
- Keep dark hair and clothing chromatic with indigo, violet-brown, blue-green, olive, or reflected warmth instead of flat black.
- Match paint chronology across the scene: underpaint, broad masses, broken middle strokes, and sparse accents should remain visible on people as well as their surroundings.
- Vary mark geometry by material. Skin, hair, fabric, water, foliage, and architecture must not share one universal impasto stamp.
- Use cast shadows and reflections as broken color connections between a person and the ground or water, not as separate hard-edged duplicates.

## Visual Lessons From the Approved Studies

- **Waterside crowd:** many small people remain readable as irregular vertical dark-light clumps, hats, and gestures; water and boats receive more description than anatomy.
- **Garden promenade:** larger seated and standing figures retain pose through hats, umbrellas, broad clothing planes, and silhouette while faces stay economical.
- **Poppy field:** figures dissolve most strongly into air and vegetation; a blue parasol, dark dress, or warm hat carries recognition more effectively than anatomy.
- **Atmospheric meadow figure:** the meadow is established as a few connected green, rust, violet, and muted-flower masses before sparse accents appear; the single figure keeps pose, white dress, dark hair mass, bag, and phone through connected planes while the distant tree line compresses into a soft low-contrast band.

Use these as behavior references only. Do not copy their compositions, clothing, gardens, boats, flags, or palettes into a new source.

## Reject and Retry

- **Protected digital figure:** the person is smooth, glossy, or airbrushed while the landscape is visibly painted.
- **Cut-out figure:** every contour is equally sharp and no environmental color crosses the silhouette.
- **Anatomy inventory:** small people have unnecessary fingers, facial features, seams, or individually drawn limbs.
- **Wardrobe inventory:** midground figures retain hair strands, polished skin shading, garment seams, printed dots, or bag texture that the scene does not need.
- **Sculpted tailoring:** a dress or coat is divided into repeated ruffles, pleats, scallops, and sharply shadowed folds, making the clothing read as crisp 3D fashion before it reads as one atmospheric mass.
- **Dead dark block:** hair or clothing collapses into flat black without chromatic interior variation.
- **Equal-detail crowd:** every person receives the same resolution regardless of distance or importance.
- **Global texture:** clothing, skin, sky, grass, and water share the same repeated ridge or tile.

Retry with:

> Preserve every person's count, placement, scale, pose, facing direction, clothing-color anchors, garment silhouette and length, and props, but stop protecting the figures as smooth digital anatomy. Allocate detail by apparent size: distant people become two-to-five connected gesture-and-value marks; midground people use broad warm-cool body planes and clothing built from two to four connected planes plus one or two fold gestures. Omit hair strands, polished skin shading, fingers, seams, printed patterns, repeated pleats, ruffles, scallops, lace, and dark tailoring grooves unless identity depends on them; only identity-critical faces receive sparse small accents. Carry ambient scene color across selected figure edges, keep dark cores chromatic, and paint skin, hair, fabric, ground, foliage, and water with different material-specific marks. Do not crop, invent, duplicate, outline, airbrush, or change the pose.
