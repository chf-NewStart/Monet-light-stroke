# Approved Benchmarks

Use approved images as visual calibration for reusable paint behavior. Preserve the new source's factual scene; never copy a benchmark's subjects, objects, crop, palette, or composition unless the user explicitly asks.

## B001 — Sunset Seabird

- **Status:** approved by the user on 2026-08-10
- **Image:** [benchmark-001-sunset-seabird.png](../assets/benchmarks/benchmark-001-sunset-seabird.png)
- **SHA-256:** `85808a47b7c6c2b741ab47c908e7c886f152b64c90013807e93b5283fdefffb9`
- **Dimensions:** 1254 × 1254
- **Use for:** layered outdoor oil-paint construction, open-sea rhythm, broken reflected light, chromatic darks, and selective impasto.

### Accepted Qualities

- Thin gray-blue and muted green ground remains visible beneath broader scumbles and broken middle strokes.
- Stroke length, direction, opacity, and loading vary by wave depth rather than repeating one global texture.
- Warm reflection contains separate yellow, ochre, orange, coral, rose, lilac, blue-gray, and cream notes interrupted by cool water.
- Thick paint is concentrated on selected glints, foam, and focal anchors; quieter passages remain thinner and canvas-bearing.
- The bird remains faithful and readable but uses blue-violet, charcoal blue, olive, violet-brown, gray, and reflected warm notes instead of a flat black cutout.
- Large wave bands and light direction remain factual while small water details are repainted freely.

### Rejected Failure Modes

- A photograph with paint texture laid over it.
- Rectangular palette-knife slabs or checkerboard color tiles.
- One smooth, digitally combed wave pattern.
- Uniform impasto or identical short dabs across the whole canvas.
- Solid premixed gold reflections and hard black subject contours.
- Invented subjects, altered subject count, flipped orientation, crop drift, or missing visible body parts.

### Transfer Rule

When applying B001 to another image, transfer only the hierarchy of layers, broken-color logic, selective thickness, chromatic edges, and controlled irregularity. Derive palette, mark direction, material behavior, subject detail, light key, and composition from the new source.

## B004 — Luminous Poplar Color Relationships

- **Status:** historical color-reference benchmark selected by the user on 2026-08-10
- **External source:** [Claude Monet, *Poplars on the Epte* (1891), National Galleries of Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/5195/poplars-river-epte-1891)
- **Distribution note:** the user-supplied screenshot is intentionally not bundled in this public repository. Its source permits personal, non-commercial image use under specific terms; keep B004 as text-only calibration until a redistributable open-access reference is selected.
- **Use for:** perceptual color tuning, selective chromatic lift, colored edges and shadows, atmosphere-wide palette recurrence, and luminous foliage, trunks, and reflections.

### Accepted Qualities

- Broad gray-lavender air remains quiet, allowing narrower coral-gold trunks, yellow-green grass, emerald foliage, and blue-violet shadow notes to appear luminous.
- Brightness is relational rather than global: only selected light-facing passages carry clearer chroma and stronger value contrast.
- Trunks are not literal brown columns; peach, ochre, coral, rose, violet-brown, and cool edge notes turn them into carriers of atmospheric light.
- Foliage separates into neighboring olive, yellow-green, emerald, blue-green, and blue-violet passages rather than one local green.
- Related colors recur across trees, air, ground, and reflection at different values and intensities, unifying the scene.
- Reflections echo the vertical rhythm and color families while becoming quieter, grayer, broken, and water-dependent.

### Transfer Rule

Transfer only the relational palette behavior: preserve the new source's light and recognizable color families, tune local hues within them, keep most of the field restrained, and concentrate clearer warm-cool accents where light or structure calls for them. Do not copy the poplars, exact palette, vertical arrangement, reflection design, signature, border, or composition.

- **Accepted SHA-256:** `6522be2337099f8d52a44f4222e0bbff4978f4bec93917fdd302bff0c22373a2`
- **Rejected comparison:** [rejected-005-overblended-cactus-dusk.png](../assets/benchmarks/rejected-005-overblended-cactus-dusk.png)
- **Rejected SHA-256:** `c43a48430901cb5fc733d380e735e300072c6a7ee9a5046ec368d363ace9e50e`
- **Dimensions:** 1086 × 1448 for both
- **Use for:** material-routed coarseness, separated dusk-sky color, optical vibration, vertical cactus structure, simplified village planes, broken foreground ground, and chromatic twilight shadows.

### Accepted Qualities

- The sky is built from visibly separated gray-blue, lavender, rose, coral, peach, and muted-orange marks rather than a seamless gradient. Broad and medium passages overlap while retaining enough raw separation for optical mixing.
- Bold upper-sky marks remain distinct from the cacti's elongated vertical rib strokes, the town's compressed planar marks, and the ground's clustered dragged strokes; the image passes the material-transfer test.
- Paint thickness and edge energy vary across the scene instead of flattening into one uniform surface.
- Cactus and foreground shadows remain dark but contain blue, violet, olive, reflected coral, warm-gray, and muted atmospheric touches that prevent dead near-black weight.
- Clear coral sky notes and sparse village lights create luminosity relationally against quieter gray-blue and chromatic-dark fields.
- The factual crop, orientation, cactus arrangement, skyline, mountain ridge, and light direction remain faithful to the source.

### Rejected Comparison

The smoother revision is factually coherent but loses the preferred painting logic:

- Its blue-gray-to-coral sky transition is more seamlessly blended, reducing visible color separation and optical vibration.
- Its upper sky has a flatter, more uniform surface and fewer decisive paint events.
- Its cactus and foreground shadows feel heavier because fewer ambient blue, violet, and reflected warm notes remain visible.
- It demonstrates that a cleaner, calmer revision can be a regression when it removes an approved passage's painterly energy.

### Transfer Rule

Transfer B005's hierarchy, not its scene: keep distinct but overlapping atmospheric color notes, route strokes by material, preserve internally chromatic dark masses, and resist smoothing an approved energetic passage into conventional realism. Derive the subjects, crop, exact palette, sky pattern, cactus forms, town, and light key from the new source.

## Cross-Benchmark Preference

When choosing between two factually correct results, prefer the version whose secondary sky, ground, sand, water, foliage, or distant architecture reads unmistakably as worked paint. Do not replace expressive coarse passages with cleaner precision merely because the latter is smoother or more orderly.

When color feels more literal but less alive in one result, prefer selective relational tuning over a global saturation or exposure increase: quieter atmospheric fields, a few cleaner chromatic accents, colored shadows, and recurring warm-cool notes across materials.

Reject any result that fails the material-transfer test: if a representative stroke patch could move unchanged between sky, water, ground, foliage, architecture, or cactus and remain plausible, the coarseness is generic rather than descriptive.

When B005 and its rejected comparison are relevant, prefer the accepted image's separated sky marks and chromatic shadow weave. Do not let an automatic cleanup pass replace optical vibration with a seamless gradient merely because the result appears calmer or more polished.
